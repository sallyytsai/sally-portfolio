I am an MPA/ID candidate at Harvard Kennedy School (2027), specializing in international political economy, trade policy, and causal inference. My research asks how small open economies navigate the intersection of security competition and economic integration — particularly when the two pull in opposite directions. I work primarily with cross-national quantitative methods and combine them with process tracing and case analysis grounded in the political and institutional context.

I hold a concurrent appointment as a Research Affiliate at the Philippine Competition Commission (with Carlos Vega), where I study fiscal pass-through in regulated markets. Before HKS, I worked in public policy and finance in Taiwan and Southeast Asia.

Featured Research

The Security Premium: Why Taiwan Maintains the World’s Most Restrictive Policy toward Chinese Electric Vehicles

Co-authors: Cheng Wang · Luis Hong Sanchez · HKS DPI-410, Spring 2026

Methods: Cross-national ordered logit (N ≈ 60) · Synthetic control · Placebo inference · Process tracing

Taiwan bans BYD outright while Germany imposes a 35% tariff and Indonesia mandates local content. Why do countries facing the same Chinese EV competitive threat respond so differently? This paper argues the key variable is security salience — the degree to which a country perceives the exporting state as a strategic threat — rather than domestic industry lobbying or consumer welfare calculus.

We construct a 0–3 ordered policy restrictiveness index across 60+ countries and show security salience (operationalized as UNGA divergence, alliance portfolio distance, and SCO membership) predicts the upper tail of the distribution even after controlling for domestic EV penetration, production capacity, and democratic accountability. A synthetic control for Taiwan traces the 2022–2024 tightening and rejects alternative explanations. Process tracing confirms the mechanism runs through elite threat perception, not industry rents.

Framework: The paper maps countries onto a 2×2 of (security salience × domestic EV industry strength) to distinguish four policy archetypes: Security Firewall (Taiwan), Strategic Hedge (EU), Industrial Shield (Indonesia/Thailand), and Open Market (Singapore/NZ).

VAT Pass-Through in Philippine Pharmaceutical Markets: Evidence from the TRAIN Act

Co-author: Carlos Vega (Philippine Competition Commission / University of East Anglia) · Working Paper, 2026

Methods: Difference-in-differences · Triple-difference · Synthetic control · Welfare decomposition

The Philippines’ 2018 TRAIN Act removed VAT exemptions on medicines for non-essential conditions while maintaining them for drugs treating diabetes, hypertension, and high cholesterol. This natural experiment allows clean identification of VAT pass-through rates under market regulation.

Using transaction-level pharmacy data, we estimate near-complete pass-through (≈95%) for affected molecules, implying the tax burden falls almost entirely on consumers. A triple-difference exploiting the essential/non-essential split and the brand/generic margin confirms the finding is not driven by concurrent supply shocks. Synthetic control corroborates the parallel-trends assumption. Welfare calculations show the bottom income quintile bears approximately 2.3× the VAT burden as a share of out-of-pocket health expenditure relative to the top quintile.

The results have direct implications for pharmaceutical pricing regulation and VAT exemption design in middle-income countries with mixed public-private drug markets.

Geopolitical Dutch Disease: Does Security Spending Crown Out Tradable Non-Defense Sectors? A panel IV approach testing whether elevated defense spending in small open economies depresses export competitiveness in non-defense manufacturing, using geopolitical risk shocks as instruments.
