I study how small open economies — particularly Taiwan — design industrial and trade policy under geopolitical pressure. My current work spans two empirical projects: a cross-national analysis of how security salience shapes trade restrictions on geopolitical rivals’ goods, with Taiwan’s EV ban as the crucial case; and a study of VAT pass-through in Philippine pharmaceutical markets, where procurement regulation suppresses the consumer welfare gains from fiscal reform. A third project examines how Taiwanese SMEs adjust supply chains and export markets under sustained geopolitical disruption. I combine quantitative methods with Mandarin-language primary source access — MOEA firm data, SMEA surveys, Legislative Yuan records — that most researchers working on these questions cannot use directly.

Currently: MPA/ID Candidate 2027, Harvard Kennedy School. Consultant (Intern), African Development Bank — Africa Legal Support Facility (ALSF), Jun–Sep 2026. Former Economics Intern, Philippine Competition Commission, Economics Office.

Featured Research

The Security Premium: Why Taiwan Maintains the World’s Most Restrictive Policy toward Chinese Electric Vehicles

Authors: Sally Tsai · Cheng Wang · Luis Hong Sanchez Venue: Harvard Kennedy School, DPI-410: The Politics of Development · Spring 2026

Methods: Cross-national ordered logit (N=48) · Synthetic control · Within-case process trace · Leave-one-out cross-validation · Placebo inference

Taiwan produces 275,000 vehicles annually, hosts no domestically owned EV brand, and has no organized producer lobby — the Grossman-Helpman model predicts minimal trade restriction. Its observed policy is a complete ban on Chinese vehicles, the most restrictive of any economy in the world. We argue the missing variable is security salience: when the import source is a geopolitical adversary capable of weaponizing economic interdependence, governments apply a security premium above the Grossman-Helpman equilibrium.

Three methods triangulate on this conclusion. A cross-national ordered logit across 60+ countries shows Taiwan is the largest positive residual in the industry-only model; when the EU is correctly treated as a single policy decision-maker, the security channel achieves statistical significance (α = −0.524, SE = 0.231, p<0.05). A leave-one-out cross-validation estimates only a 4% probability that Taiwan would receive maximum restriction absent the security channel. A synthetic control places Taiwan’s vehicle production 29% below its counterfactual trajectory after 2016. A within-case process trace provides mechanism identification: policy tightening began only when the DPP raised security salience after 2016 — the same structural threat under the KMT from 2008–2016 produced no additional restriction, isolating political salience as the operative variable rather than structural threat level.

The paper introduces a 2×2 framework (ICE industry size × EV adoption level) that generates quadrant-specific predictions for 193 countries, identifying where the Grossman-Helpman channel dominates, where the security channel is necessary and sufficient, and where consumer welfare overwhelms both. Taiwan in Q3 (small ICE industry, low EV adoption, maximum restriction) is the cleanest test case for the security premium.

Policy implication: As Chinese EV manufacturers expand globally, the security-augmented model predicts the frontier of trade restriction will be determined by security alignment, not domestic industry size.

VAT Pass-Through in Philippine Pharmaceutical Markets: Evidence from the TRAIN Act

Authors: Sally Tsai · Carlos Vega Affiliations: Harvard Kennedy School; Philippine Competition Commission / Centre for Competition Policy, University of East Anglia · June 2026

Methods: Difference-in-differences (product FE, 8 pre-treatment quarters, event study) · Triple-difference by license type · Synthetic control (ASEAN comparators) · Parallel quantity DiD

The 2019 TRAIN Act (RA 10963) exempted essential medicines for hypertension, diabetes, and high cholesterol from the Philippines’ 12% value-added tax — a reform explicitly designed to lower out-of-pocket drug costs for Filipino households. Using product-level quarterly IQVIA MIDAS data (2009–2021, N=44,339 observations, 1,953 products), we find no pass-through to patients: treated drug prices rose approximately 2.3% relative to controls following the exemption (β̂ = 0.023***, SE = 0.005), with firms absorbing the tax relief entirely as margin expansion on unregulated low-volume products. Volume-weighted estimates are statistically indistinguishable from zero, consistent with procurement regulation suppressing pass-through for the high-volume products that dominate patient spending.

A triple-difference decomposition by license type reveals the competitive mechanism. Originators with inelastic brand-loyal demand extracted the full tax relief as margin (β̂_O = 0.045***); branded non-originators showed a significantly smaller response (β̂_B = 0.013**); unbranded generics facing price competition showed no significant adjustment (β̂_U = 0.002, n.s.). Wald tests confirm the originator–generic gradient is statistically distinct (p<0.001). A parallel DiD on quantities finds retail volumes of treated drugs fell approximately 20% (β̂ = −0.208***, SE = 0.063) while hospital volumes were unchanged — a double welfare loss in the retail channel: list prices rose and retail consumption fell simultaneously. Back-of-envelope welfare accounting finds Filipino households incurred roughly PHP 216–262 more in annual essential drug costs than they would have saved under full pass-through — approximately 4–5% of baseline out-of-pocket spending.

Policy implication: VAT exemptions are an ineffective instrument for reducing patient drug costs when procurement regulation already binds for high-volume products. The fiscal cost accrues to producers, not patients.

Research in Progress

SME Trade Adjustment under Geopolitical Pressure (SYPA — in progress)

How do Taiwanese SMEs adjust export destinations and supply chains under sustained geopolitical pressure from China? Identification exploits three distinct escalation episodes — the 2016 DPP election, 2019–20 trade tensions, and the 2022 Pelosi visit and PLA exercises — as temporal variation in treatment intensity, using staggered DiD (Callaway-Sant’Anna) on MOEA Industry Statistics and SMEA Annual Survey firm data. New Southbound Policy subsidies provide a natural treatment-control comparison for identifying intentional diversification responses versus passive adjustment.
